## Key point 6 — The "how," not just the "why"

Duration alone doesn't explain who makes it and who quietly stops. Gollwitzer, P. M. (1999). *Implementation intentions: Strong effects of simple plans.* American Psychologist, 54(7), 493–503.

Reword the goal: not "I'll shower more," but "when I get back from the gym, I will shower before I sit down." That single change roughly doubles the odds it actually happens. A real plan needs a time, not an intention.

<CheckIn question="Rewrite one of your own vague goals as an if-then plan with a stated time." />

## Key point 7 — Your starting point

Before the self-assessment: look at your own desk. If its usable surface has quietly become a filing system for things you'll deal with later, that's not a character flaw — it's the first data point.

The Week 1 lab runs the Starting Point Check: an honest self-assessment across the course's five recurring topics — hygiene, dress, sleep, conversation, and money — scored against what you actually do rather than what you'd like to do.

<CheckIn question="Looking at your own desk right now, is its usable surface storage or workspace?" />
