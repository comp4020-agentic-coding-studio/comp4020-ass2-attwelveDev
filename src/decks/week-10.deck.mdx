## Definitions

- **Budget** (noun) — a named allocation of money to specific categories, prepared in advance of spending, not reconstructed afterward from bank statements.
- **Grocery list** (noun) — a written record of food to purchase, prepared before entering a supermarket, not while standing in it.

---

## Key point 1 — Why naming categories matters

Warren, E., & Warren Tyagi, A. (2005). *All Your Worth: The Ultimate Lifetime Money Plan.* Free Press.

The 50/30/20 rule: 50% of after-tax income to needs, 30% to wants, 20% to savings or debt.

Three named categories, not an unstructured sense of caution.

## Key point 3 — Cooking a complete meal, in order

Pick one protein, one starch, one vegetable — that's a complete meal, not three separate decisions.

Cook in order of how long each takes: slowest on first, fastest on last, so everything finishes within a few minutes of each other.

Taste before declaring it done — a recipe's stated time is a starting guess, not a guarantee.

<CheckIn question="If rice takes 20 minutes and a fried egg takes 3, which goes on the stove first?" />

## Key point 4 — A cleaning schedule that actually recurs

Same model as Week 2's laundry schedule: name each recurring task — bins, floors, bathroom, surfaces.

Give each a fixed day or interval, not "when it looks dirty."

A schedule catches a task before it's visible; waiting for visible means it's already overdue.

<CheckIn question="Which of your own recurring cleaning tasks currently has no fixed day attached to it?" />

## Key point 5 — What Assignment 3's budget section actually wants

Two marked criteria: Budget (12%) marks category coverage and plausibility against your stated income.

Grocery logistics (6%) marks a one-week grocery list against an explicit grocery budget, plausible against real prices, with a standing schedule for when recurring items run out.

Neither rewards a bigger number — both reward a number that survives contact with a real week.

<CheckIn question="Between Budget and Grocery logistics, which one is actually marking whether your numbers are realistic against real prices?" />
