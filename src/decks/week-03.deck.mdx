## Definitions

- **Outfit** (noun) — a coordinated set of garments selected for a specific occasion, as distinct from "whatever was on the floor."
- **Dress code** (noun) — an unwritten or written expectation for attire at a given event, violation of which is noticed by everyone except the violator.
- **Occasion** (noun) — an event with its own implicit clothing requirements, distinct from "any day that ends in Y."
- **Job interview** (noun) — a short, structured meeting in which a candidate is evaluated for a role; covered in full in Week 11.

## Key point 1 — Clothing changes cognition, not just perception

Adam, H., & Galinsky, A. D. (2012). *Enclothed cognition.* Journal of Experimental Social Psychology, 48(4), 918–925.

A coat described as a doctor's lab coat measurably improved sustained attention versus the same coat described as a painter's coat.

The effect needs both the physical experience of wearing it and its symbolic meaning.

## Key point 2 — Reading an occasion, check one

What formality does the event itself require?

A lecture reads casual. A gym session reads athletic (exercise itself is Week 4's topic). An interview (Week 11) reads formal.

The same item can be right or wrong depending on what it's read against — not on the item alone.

<CheckIn question="What's the one detail that would flip an outfit from interview-appropriate to gym-appropriate?" />

---

## Key point 3 — Reading an occasion, check two

Weather and season count as much as occasion does.

A linen shirt and light jacket read as correctly casual for a lecture on a mild day.

The same outfit is wrong in a summer heatwave — soaked through before you arrive — or a winter cold snap with nothing that blocks wind.

An outfit that passes the occasion check and fails the weather check is still the wrong outfit.

<CheckIn question="Name one outfit that would pass the occasion check but fail if today turned out cold or wet." />

## Key point 4 — Assembling from what you own

List your own recurring occasion types once — lectures, gym, one social event a week — and settle on one reliable outfit per type.

A wardrobe only has to cover a small number of recurring occasion types, not one item per occasion. Assignment 1 previews the same task.

<CheckIn question="How many genuinely different occasion types does your own week actually contain?" />

## In-lecture activity

Two outfit photos, side by side.

Vote by show of hands: which is interview-appropriate?

Same two photos — now, which is gym-appropriate?

The point is the answer changes with the question, not with the photos.

## Conclusion

What you wear changes how you perform, not just how you're seen.

The closest thing in this course to a genuine hack.

You can now run both checks — occasion first, then weather — and assemble an outfit from a wardrobe that only has to cover a handful of recurring types.
