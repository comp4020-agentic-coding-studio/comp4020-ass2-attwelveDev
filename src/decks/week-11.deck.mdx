## Definitions

- **Interview** (noun) — a structured conversation in which a candidate's suitability for a role is assessed via prepared questions, not vibes.
- **Professional email** (noun) — a written workplace message with one clearly stated purpose, ideally identifiable without reading past the second sentence.
- **Meeting** (noun) — a scheduled gathering of people who could have been sent an email, distinguished from one by ending without anyone confirming who does what by when.

## Key point 1 — Structured answers beat improvised ones

Schmidt, F. L., & Hunter, J. E. (1998). *The validity and utility of selection methods in personnel psychology.* Psychological Bulletin, 124(2), 262–274.

Structured interviews: predictive validity of about .51 for job performance.

Combined with a general mental ability measure: about .63.

## Key point 2 — Structured conduct in meetings

Decide your one input before the meeting starts — a point to make, or a question to ask. Contribute it early, not just when asked.

Disagree with the structure from Week 8's apology model in reverse: state the alternative and the evidence for it, not just "I don't think that's right."

Leave with a decided next step, named with an owner and a date — a meeting that ends without one produced nothing, however long it ran.

<CheckIn question="What's the difference between disagreeing with 'I don't think that's right' and disagreeing with an alternative plus evidence?" />

## Key point 3 — Why the bad email fails

The email you just heard, in three problems: a buried ask, a rambling opening, an irrelevant tangent about lunch plans.

The same overestimation of how clearly the words alone were landing — Week 9's Kruger et al. (2005) finding on egocentrism in text, again.

<CheckIn question="What are the actual three things this email needed to say, once everything else is cut?" />

---

## Key point 4 — The three-sentence rule, applied

State the situation, state the ask, state what you need from the reader — one sentence each, in that order.

Applied here: "The deadline needs to move by one to two days. Can everyone confirm that works by tomorrow? If not, let me know what would."

Everything else — the greeting, the apology for being slow, the lunch tangent — goes in a separate message, if at all.
