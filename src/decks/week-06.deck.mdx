## Key point 1 — The mere-presence effect

Ward, A. F., Duke, K., Gneezy, A., & Bos, M. W. (2017). *Brain Drain: The Mere Presence of One's Own Smartphone Reduces Available Cognitive Capacity.* Journal of the Association for Consumer Research, 2(2), 140–154.

Phones on the desk — even face-down, even switched off — meant worse memory and attention performance than phones in another room.

The effect scaled with how noticeable the phone was.

---

## Key point 2 — Why this isn't a willpower failure

A measurable effect of where the phone sits. Not a personal failing.

The same no-blame reading this course applies to Week 8's incident reports.

<CheckIn question="If the effect is about where the phone sits, what does that suggest about fixing it with willpower alone?" />

---

## Key point 3 — Designing an enforcement mechanism

Where the laptop physically goes matters more than deciding not to use it — another room, not just another desk.

Name a device-free window with a start time and an end time.

Decide in advance what happens if the laptop comes back before the window ends, and allow exactly one exception: a genuine, already-known deadline, named ahead of time.
