## Definitions

- **Shower** (noun) — a fixture or enclosure in which a person stands to wash the body using a controlled spray of water, typically once per calendar day.
- **Deodorant** (noun) — a substance applied to the underarms to reduce detectable odour, typically reapplied daily.
- **Laundry** (noun, uncountable) — clothing and linens requiring washing — also, the act of washing them, ideally before the pile is visible from the doorway.
- **Haircut** (noun) — a scheduled trim of head hair, recommended at an interval measured in weeks, not academic semesters.
- **Skincare** (noun) — the practice of maintaining and improving the health, barrier function, and appearance of the skin.

## Key point 1 — Frequency, not intention

Aiello, A. E., Coulborn, R. M., Perez, V., & Larson, E. L. (2008). *Effect of hand hygiene on infectious disease risk in the community setting: A meta-analysis.* American Journal of Public Health, 98(8), 1372–1381.

Improved hand hygiene reduced gastrointestinal illness by 31% and respiratory illness by 21% across 30 pooled studies.

## Key point 2 — Anchoring the daily basics

Shower once a day, in the same slot as something you already do without deciding — right after waking, or right before bed.

Deodorant goes on straight after, to dry skin, before getting dressed. Wet skin means it wipes off on the towel instead of doing anything.

Haircuts: book the next appointment on the day of the last one, or set a standing reminder every four to six weeks. Decide the interval once, not every time it looks long.

## Key point 3 — The laundry pile as a schedule

Twenty-three unpaired socks, collected from under a bed, a desk, and the back seat of a car.

Every one is proof laundry got deferred past the point a matching pair survived — not proof of a personality trait.

A schedule fixes the pile; keeping colours separate — whites in one load, everything else in another — fixes the pink towel.

Week 12 returns to this exact pile.

<CheckIn question="Is your current laundry pile a schedule, or an event that happens to you?" />

## Key point 4 — A skincare routine, step by step

Cleanse the face once in the morning, once at night — a cleanser meant for skin, not the bar soap from the shower.

Moisturise straight after, while the face is still damp. That's what stops the tight, dry feeling, not the cleanser.

Mornings only: sunscreen, SPF 30 or higher, cloudy days included.

Three products, in that order, under five minutes.
