## Definitions

- **Bedtime** (noun) — a specific, named clock time at which one intends to be asleep, not merely "later."
- **Balanced meal** (noun) — a plate containing more than one food group, particularly one that is not currently instant noodles.
- **Exercise** (noun) — physical activity undertaken on a stated schedule, distinguished from "the walk to the vending machine."

## Key point 1 — How much sleep you actually need

Hirshkowitz, M., Whiton, K., Albert, S. M., et al. (2015). *National Sleep Foundation's sleep time duration recommendations: methodology and results summary.* Sleep Health, 1(1), 40–43.

Seven to nine hours recommended for adults 18–64.

## Key point 2 — How to actually get there

Two things: a wake time fixed to the same clock time every day, weekends included.

And a screen wind-down that starts at a named time before bed — not "whenever I feel tired."

## Key point 5 — Exercise as a frequency, not a location

Chekroud, S. R., Gueorguieva, R., Zheutlin, A. B., Paulus, M., Krumholz, H. M., Krystal, J. H., & Chekroud, A. M. (2018). *Association between physical exercise and mental health in 1.2 million individuals in the USA between 2011 and 2015.* The Lancet Psychiatry, 5(9), 739–746.

1.49 fewer poor-mental-health days per month than non-exercisers. Benefits peaked around 45 minutes, 3–5 times a week.

More than 3 hours a day was associated with worse outcomes than none at all.

<CheckIn question="According to this study, is more exercise always better? What does the data actually say?" />

---

## Key point 6 — A beginner routine that hits that range

Three sessions a week, 20–30 minutes each, at a stated time slot.

Any activity that raises your heart rate counts — a gym session is one option, not the only one.

A brisk walk on a fixed route satisfies the same frequency.

---

## Key point 7 — The fridge, revisited

Week 2's fridge shelf hasn't changed: condiments, one ageing lime, still nothing that constitutes a meal.

Which is exactly why Wednesday's plan collapses into the same bowl of instant noodles twice in a day.

A sleep schedule and an exercise plan both assume there's something to eat between them — an empty fridge breaks both.

<CheckIn question="What's the direct link between an empty fridge and Wednesday's two servings of instant noodles?" />

---

## Key point 8 — How to actually build a balanced meal

Three items on the plate, at the same sitting, not spread across the day: a base (rice, pasta, bread), a protein (eggs, canned beans, last night's leftover chicken), and something that isn't beige (a vegetable — any vegetable, frozen counts).

A piece of fruit eaten four hours after the noodles doesn't retroactively balance the noodles.

The only way the fridge from Week 2 supplies all three on a Wednesday is if it was stocked on the weekend before — checking for the first time on Wednesday night is checking too late.

## Conclusion

A plan has a time; an intention doesn't. More isn't always better.

The topic most students believe they're managing and visibly aren't.

You now have the real recommended range, a fixed wake time and wind-down to get there, a beginner exercise routine that isn't tied to any one location, the fridge as a reminder that a schedule needs something to actually run on, and the three-item test for whether what's on the plate actually counts as a meal.
