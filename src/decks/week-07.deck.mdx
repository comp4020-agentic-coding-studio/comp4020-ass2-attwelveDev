## Definitions

- **Small talk** (noun) — brief, low-stakes conversation exchanged with someone who is not a close friend, typically covering weather, weekend plans, or transit delays.
- **Eye contact** (noun) — the act of looking at another person's eyes during conversation, for a duration long enough to signal attention and short enough to avoid alarm.
- **Reading the room** (verb phrase) — noticing a conversation partner's disengagement cues in time to act on them, rather than after the conversation has already ended.

## Key point 1 — The actual comfortable length of eye contact

Binetti, N., Harrison, C., Coutrot, A., Johnston, A., & Mareschal, I. (2016). *Pupil dilation as an index of preferred mutual gaze duration.* Royal Society Open Science, 3(7), 160086.

Average preferred mutual gaze duration was around 3.2–3.3 seconds.

Consistent across a wide international sample.

## Key point 3 — The small-talk formula

Name. One hobby. One recent trip.

Three fixed slots, filled in any order.

<CheckIn question="Which of the three slots is easiest to fill with a stranger, and which one usually needs the other person to ask first?" />

## Key point 4 — Reading the room, diagnose then respond

Three specific cues mean a conversation has stalled, not just paused: one-word answers where a sentence was expected, repeated glances at the door, or checking a watch mid-sentence.

Diagnose first — which cue, and is it just one.

Then respond with an exit, not another question: thank them for their time, name a reason to go, and leave.

<CheckIn question="Of the three cues, which one is easiest to miss because it doesn't involve saying anything?" />
