## Introduction

The fridge's third and final appearance: price out what's actually in it
against what a week of groceries should cost. Budgeting failure is rarely
about income — usually about the absence of named categories. Today's
scope is basic budget categorisation and grocery planning only, the most
transferable topic in the course beyond graduation.

## Body

- **Why naming categories matters.** Warren, E., & Warren Tyagi, A.
  (2005) popularised the 50/30/20 rule: 50% of after-tax income to needs,
  30% to wants, 20% to savings or debt — three named categories rather
  than an unstructured sense of caution.

<CheckIn question="If your weekly budget is $200, what does 50/30/20 look like in actual dollars?" />

- **The fridge, priced out.** Its contents, set against a real, planned
  weekly grocery list, show the gap between what's in the fridge and
  what a week actually costs to run properly.
- **What Assignment 3 actually wants.** A preview of the budget
  component of the HD-band criteria.
