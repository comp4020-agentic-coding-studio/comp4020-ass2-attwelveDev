## Introduction

The fridge's third and final appearance: price out what's actually in it
against what a week of groceries should cost. Budgeting failure is rarely
about income — usually about the absence of named categories. Today's
scope is basic budget categorisation, grocery planning, cooking, and a
cleaning schedule, the most transferable topic in the course beyond
graduation.

## Body

- **Why naming categories matters.** Warren, E., & Warren Tyagi, A.
  (2005) popularised the 50/30/20 rule: 50% of after-tax income to needs,
  30% to wants, 20% to savings or debt — three named categories rather
  than an unstructured sense of caution.

<CheckIn question="If your weekly budget is $200, what does 50/30/20 look like in actual dollars?" />

- **The fridge, priced out.** Its contents, set against a real, planned
  weekly grocery list, show the gap between what's in the fridge and
  what a week actually costs to run properly.

<CheckIn question="Roughly how much would it cost to replace the fridge's actual contents with one week of planned groceries?" />

- **Cooking a complete meal, in order.** Pick one protein, one starch,
  one vegetable — that's a complete meal, not three separate decisions.
  Cook in order of how long each takes: the slowest item goes on first,
  the fastest goes on last, so everything finishes within a few minutes
  of each other. Taste before declaring it done; a recipe's stated time
  is a starting guess, not a guarantee.

<CheckIn question="If rice takes 20 minutes and a fried egg takes 3, which goes on the stove first?" />

- **A cleaning schedule that actually recurs.** The same model as Week 2's
  laundry schedule: name each recurring task — bins, floors, bathroom,
  surfaces — and give each a fixed day or interval, not "when it looks
  dirty." A schedule catches a task before it's visible; waiting for
  visible means it's already overdue.

<CheckIn question="Which of your own recurring cleaning tasks currently has no fixed day attached to it?" />

- **What Assignment 3's budget section actually wants.** Two marked
  criteria: Budget (10%) marks category coverage and plausibility against
  your stated income; Grocery logistics (8%) marks a one-week grocery
  list against an explicit grocery budget, plausible against real prices,
  with a standing schedule for when recurring items run out. Neither
  rewards a bigger number — both reward a number that survives contact
  with a real week.

<CheckIn question="Between Budget and Grocery logistics, which one is actually marking whether your numbers are realistic against real prices?" />

## Conclusion

Categories, not totals, make a budget work — the clearest lifetime
payoff of any topic in this course. You now have a real, priced grocery
list template, a named-category method, a meal you can actually cook in
order, and a cleaning schedule with a fixed day for every task. Coming
