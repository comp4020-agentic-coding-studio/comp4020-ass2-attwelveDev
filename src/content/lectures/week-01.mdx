## Introduction

A prior cohort's anonymised exit surveys turn up the same handful of gaps
every year: one student who learned laundry from a video the night before
moving out, one who had never had to introduce themselves to a stranger,
one who thought "adulting" was a joke until it wasn't. Today is diagnostic
only — nothing is taught yet, only baselined — because every topic this
semester predicts things that matter well beyond university.

## Body

- **How lectures, labs, and reflections fit together.** The lecture
  explains why a topic matters and what the research says; the lab that
  same week puts it into practice; the reflection due before the next
  lecture makes it personal and ongoing. Each piece does something the
  others don't.

<CheckIn question="If you only ever came to labs and skipped lectures, which part of this course would you be missing entirely?" />

- **How the course is assessed.** Five components: Weekly Reflections
  (15%), Assignment 1 — Makeover (15%), Assignment 2 — Touch Grass Field
  Study (20%), Assignment 3 — Time to Be an Adult (20%), Final Exam (30%).
  Assignment 3's HD band is worth reading early — it's the funniest
  artefact in the course.

<CheckIn question="Which single assessment is worth the most of your final grade?" />

- **Staff and policies, briefly.** Submissions are paper-based, handed to
  the convenor's office or a tutor, with email accepted only for
  extenuating circumstances. No laptops or screens in any lab, ever.

<CheckIn question="Under what circumstance is emailing your submission actually allowed?" />

- **Why these skills, and why now.** Lally, P., van Jaarsveld, C. H. M.,
  Potts, H. W. W., & Wardle, J. (2010) found habits take an average of 66
  days to form, ranging from 18 to 254 — nowhere near the "just do it for
  a few days" most people expect. Duration alone doesn't explain why some
  people get there and others quietly stop: Gollwitzer, P. M. (1999) found
  that rewording a vague goal into a specific if-then plan — not "I'll
  shower more" but "when I get back from the gym, I will shower before I
  sit down" — roughly doubles the chance it actually happens. A real plan
  needs a time, not an intention.

<CheckIn question="Show of hands — who's tried to build a new habit in under a week and given up? That's what this study explains." />

- **Your starting point.** Before the self-assessment, look at your own
  desk: if its usable surface has quietly become a filing system for
  things you'll deal with later, that's not a character flaw, it's just
  the first data point. The Week 1 lab runs the Starting Point Check —
  your honest self-assessment across the course's five recurring topics:
  hygiene, dress, sleep, conversation, and money.

## Conclusion

This course treats these skills as real, teachable, and assessed — and
the lecture, lab, and reflection each do distinct, complementary work.
Competence is a skill like any other; CS culture just never taught you
this one. You now know how the course runs, how it's assessed, its
policies, and where you're starting. Coming up: this week's lab (Triage
and the Starting Point Check).

that you've stopped noticing — and describe what it would take to pay
attention to it properly for a week.
