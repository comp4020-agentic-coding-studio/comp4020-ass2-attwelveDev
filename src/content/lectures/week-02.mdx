## Body

- **Frequency, not intention.** Aiello, A. E., Coulborn, R. M., Perez, V.,
  & Larson, E. L. (2008) found improved hand hygiene reduced
  gastrointestinal illness by 31% and respiratory illness by 21% across 30
  pooled studies — a large effect from a routine, not from wanting it more.

<CheckIn question="If washing your hands more often cuts your odds of getting sick by a fifth to a third, what does that suggest about the rest of this list?" />

- **The laundry pile as a schedule, not a personality trait.** Twenty-three
  unpaired socks, collected from under a bed, a desk, and the back seat of
  a car, are proof that laundry got deferred past the point a matching
  pair survived — not proof of a character flaw. Week 12 returns to this
  exact pile.

<CheckIn question="Is your current laundry pile a schedule, or an event that happens to you?" />

- **What "good enough" looks like.** Assignment 1's rubric doesn't ask for
  a perfect routine, just one that survives a bad week.

<CheckIn question="What's the smallest version of your routine that still counts as done?" />

## Conclusion

A schedule beats a feeling — this is the cheapest topic in the course to
fix, and it compounds. You now have a real calendar template, not just an
intention. Coming up: this week's lab (the Maintenance Calendar).
