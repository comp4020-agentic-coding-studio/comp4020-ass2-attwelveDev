## Introduction

A fridge shelf, photographed monthly for a semester, holds condiments and
one ageing lime — nothing that constitutes a meal. It will come back in
weeks 4 and 10. This week's scope is narrower: showering, hair washing,
deodorant, laundry frequency, and skincare only. Each one gets a concrete
routine to run, not just a definition of what it is. It's the most
immediately fixable topic in the course.

## Body

- **Frequency, not intention.** Aiello, A. E., Coulborn, R. M., Perez, V.,
  & Larson, E. L. (2008) found improved hand hygiene reduced
  gastrointestinal illness by 31% and respiratory illness by 21% across 30
  pooled studies — a large effect from a routine, not from wanting it more.

<CheckIn question="If washing your hands more often cuts your odds of getting sick by a fifth to a third, what does that suggest about the rest of this list?" />

- **Anchoring the daily basics.** Shower once a day, in the same slot as
  something you already do without deciding — right after waking, or
  right before bed — so it stops being a fresh decision each time.
  Apply deodorant straight after, to dry skin, before getting dressed;
  wet skin means it wipes off on the towel instead of doing anything.
  For haircuts, book the next appointment on the day of the last one, or
  set a standing reminder every four to six weeks — decide the interval
  once, not every time it starts looking long.

<CheckIn question="Which of shower, deodorant, or haircut currently has no fixed time or interval attached to it?" />

- **The laundry pile as a schedule, not a personality trait.** Twenty-three
  unpaired socks, collected from under a bed, a desk, and the back seat of
  a car, are proof that laundry got deferred past the point a matching
  pair survived — not proof of a character flaw. Week 12 returns to this
  exact pile. A schedule fixes the pile; keeping colours separate on wash
  day — whites in one load, everything else in another — fixes the pink
  towel that colours the whole load.

<CheckIn question="Is your current laundry pile a schedule, or an event that happens to you?" />

- **A skincare routine, step by step.** Cleanse the face once in the
  morning and once at night, with a cleanser meant for skin rather than
  the bar soap from the shower. Moisturise straight after, while the face
  is still damp — this is what actually stops the tight, dry feeling, not
  the cleanser. In the morning only, finish with a sunscreen of at least
  SPF 30, on cloudy days too. Cleanse twice a day, moisturise twice a day,
  SPF once a day: three products, in that order, done in under five
  minutes.

<CheckIn question="Of the three skincare steps, which one is most often the one people skip?" />

- **What "good enough" looks like.** Assignment 1 marks a hygiene product
  selection (40%) on whether you can state why you chose each product over
  the alternatives on the shelf, three outfit photos (40%) on whether each
  is actually appropriate for its occasion — lecture, birthday party, gym
  — and a written justification (20%) tying each outfit back to that
  occasion. None of it asks for a flawless routine, just one that survives
  a bad week and can defend its own choices.

## Conclusion

A schedule beats a feeling — this is the cheapest topic in the course to
fix, and it compounds. Shower, deodorant, laundry, haircuts, and skincare
each now have a fixed time or interval attached, not just a definition —
you have a real calendar template, not just an intention. Coming up:
this week's lab (the Maintenance Calendar).

**This week's reflection**, due 12:00 Tuesday of week 3: describe what
actually went wrong with your hygiene routine this week — not the routine
you intend to run, the one that actually happened — and name the smallest
change that would have prevented it.
