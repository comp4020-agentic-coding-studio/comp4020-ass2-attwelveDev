## Overview

Household logistics is a supply chain with one customer and no backup
supplier: if the order doesn't arrive, or the wrong thing arrives, the
system doesn't route around it, it just runs short. Most "I never have the
right thing in the house" complaints are a reorder-point problem, not a
willpower one.

## Content

- reorder points: ordering before you run out, not after
- reading a receipt as `telemetry` on what your household actually consumes
- household tasks as a supply chain: groceries, laundry, cleaning supplies
- combining this week's logistics with weeks 2 and 4's hygiene and
  meal-planning schedules

## Case study

Fourteen delivery receipts from the same household, itemised and laid side
by side. Twelve of the fourteen contained the exact same nine items, in the
same quantities, ordered an average of 2.3 days after the previous order
ran out. The household had, without ever writing it down, already worked
out its own reorder point — and was still paying a rush-delivery surcharge
on every single order, because nobody had turned the pattern into a
schedule.
