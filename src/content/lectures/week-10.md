## Overview

A household only has one person doing the shopping and no backup: if the
order doesn't arrive, or the wrong thing arrives, nothing else picks up
the slack, it just runs short. Most "I never have the right thing in the
house" complaints are about buying too late, not about willpower.

## Content

- ordering before you run out, not after
- reading a receipt for what your household actually consumes
- household tasks that run out on their own schedule: groceries, laundry,
  cleaning supplies
- combining this week's household tasks with weeks 2 and 4's hygiene and
  meal-planning schedules

## Case study

Fourteen delivery receipts from the same household, itemised and laid side
by side. Twelve of the fourteen contained the exact same nine items, in the
same quantities, ordered an average of 2.3 days after the previous order
ran out. The household had, without ever writing it down, already worked
out exactly when it was about to run out — and was still paying a
rush-delivery surcharge on every single order, because nobody had turned
the pattern into a schedule.
