## Overview

Small talk has an opening move and a follow-up, the same way any exchange
does: two people offer a small amount before either says anything that
matters. When neither side offers a follow-up, the conversation doesn't
stall because of unfriendliness — it stalls because nothing was said that
could actually be replied to.

## Content

- small talk as the opening move of a conversation, not filler before the
  real one
- reading a pause as normal, not as a signal to fill immediately
- what to say when the first attempt at an opening gets nothing back
- the difference between a stalled exchange and a genuinely closed one

## Case study

*"How was your weekend?" — "Yeah, good." (2:04) "You?" (2:07) "Yeah, not
bad." (2:09)* — a transcript of an actual exchange, timestamped. Both
sides said something that counted as an opening. Neither said anything the
other side could actually reply to, so the conversation started and
immediately went quiet. The failure was not unfriendliness; it was an
opening line with no follow-up attached, on both sides at once.
