## Overview

A conversation opens with a handshake, the same way a connection does: two
sides exchange a small amount of information to confirm the channel is
live before either sends anything that matters. When the handshake fails,
everything after it is built on a connection neither side actually
confirmed.

## Content

- small talk as a handshake protocol, not filler before the real
  conversation
- reading silence as latency, not as a signal to fill immediately
- retry logic: what to say when the first attempt at an opening gets
  nothing back
- the difference between a stalled exchange and a genuinely closed one

## Case study

*"How was your weekend?" — "Yeah, good." (2:04) "You?" (2:07) "Yeah, not
bad." (2:09)* — a transcript of an actual exchange, timestamped. Both
sides sent a valid opening packet. Neither sent anything the other side
could reply to, so the channel opened and immediately idled. The failure
was not unfriendliness; it was an opening line with no follow-up
attached, on both sides at once.
