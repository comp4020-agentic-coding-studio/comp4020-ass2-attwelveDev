## Body

- **Revisiting Lally et al. (2010).** A 12-week semester falls short of
  the 66-day average from Week 1 — stated honestly, not overclaimed.

<CheckIn question="Based on Week 1's research, would you expect every habit in this course to be fully formed by today?" />

- **What actually changed, and how we know.** The Then and Now method:
  pull up the five scores from Week 1's starting point check, alongside
  every schedule and plan built since — Week 2's hygiene schedule,
  Week 4's sleep and exercise plan, Week 10's shopping schedule. Pick the
  one area that changed the most, and back it up with something you
  actually did this semester, not something you meant to — the same
  starting-state/what-changed/actual-reason shape as Week 8's incident
  report, except this time the outcome is good. The point of running it
  this way: a self-report alone can't tell "I got better at this" apart
  from "I've decided I got better at this" — a schedule you actually kept
  can. This week's lab runs the method itself, using the scores and
  schedules you bring with you.

<CheckIn question="Between a self-report and a kept schedule, which one can actually tell you apart from someone who just decided they'd improved?" />

- **Closing the loop.** The fridge from weeks 2, 4, and 10 — condiments
  and one ageing lime then, a photographed week of planned groceries
  priced against a real budget now. The sock pile from week 2, down
  from twenty-three unpaired socks to four, tracked against a laundry
  schedule that finally states a frequency. Week 1's desk, its
  accumulated clutter replaced by a shallow tray with somewhere for
  things to go. Jordan's Week from week 4 — six stated intentions and
  zero actual times then; a sleep and exercise schedule that either held
  or has an honest account of where it slipped, now. The 600-word
  message from week 9 — the three-sentence rule, applied to whatever
  actually needed sending since.

<CheckIn question="Of these five callbacks, which one do you have real evidence for in your own semester — a number, a photo, a schedule — rather than just a sense that it got better?" />
