## Body

- **Structured answers beat improvised ones.** Schmidt, F. L., & Hunter,
  J. E. (1998) found structured interviews showed a predictive validity
  of about .51 for job performance, meaningfully higher than
  unstructured interviews; combined with a general mental ability
  measure, validity rose to about .63.

<CheckIn question="According to this meta-analysis, is a free-flowing, unstructured interview actually a better predictor of job performance, or worse?" />

- **Structured conduct in meetings.** Decide your one input before the
  meeting starts — a point to make or a question to ask — and contribute
  it early rather than waiting to be asked. Disagree with the structure
  from Week 8's apology model in reverse: state the alternative and the
  evidence for it, not just "I don't think that's right." Leave with a
  decided next step, named with an owner and a date — a meeting that
  ends without one produced nothing, however long it ran.

<CheckIn question="What's the difference between disagreeing with 'I don't think that's right' and disagreeing with an alternative plus evidence?" />

- **Why the bad email fails, in full.** Sent at 11:52pm on a Sunday to a
  group of five, forwarded within the hour by three separate recipients
  to the other two with no comment beyond "thoughts?", reproduced below
  exactly as it landed in all five inboxes at once:

  > Hi team,
  >
  > Hope you're all doing well! Just wanted to reach out and touch base
  > about a few things. So I've been thinking a lot about the project
  > timeline, and I know we all discussed this a bit last week, but I
  > wanted to circle back and get everyone's thoughts again since things
  > have kind of shifted a little since then. There's a lot going on this
  > week for me personally so I might be a bit slow to respond to things,
  > just a heads up. Anyway, the main thing is I think we might need an
  > extra day for the deadline, or maybe two, not totally sure yet, still
  > figuring that out. Let me know what you all think when you get a
  > chance, no rush though! Also random thought but should we get lunch
  > sometime this week? Anyway let me know about the deadline thing.
  >
  > Thanks so much,
  > [Name]

  A buried ask, a rambling opening, an irrelevant tangent about lunch
  plans — the same overestimation of how clearly the words alone were
  landing that Week 9's Kruger et al. (2005) finding on egocentrism in
  text already covered.

<CheckIn question="What are the actual three things this email needed to say, once everything else is cut?" />

- **The three-sentence rule, applied.** State the situation, state the
  ask, state what you need from the reader — one sentence each, in that
  order. Applied to the email above: "The deadline needs to move by one
  to two days. Can everyone confirm that works by tomorrow? If not, let
  me know what would." Everything else — the greeting, the apology for
  being slow, the lunch tangent — goes in a separate message, if at all.

## Conclusion

Interviews, meetings, and emails all reward structure over instinct —
the most immediate real-world stakes of any topic in this course. You
now have a structure for interview answers, a way to contribute in a
meeting and leave with a decided next step, and a three-sentence
technique for emails. Coming up: this week's lab (the mock interview
