## Body

- **Structured answers beat improvised ones.** Schmidt, F. L., & Hunter,
  J. E. (1998) found structured interviews showed a predictive validity
  of about .51 for job performance, meaningfully higher than
  unstructured interviews; combined with a general mental ability
  measure, validity rose to about .63.

<CheckIn question="According to this meta-analysis, is a free-flowing, unstructured interview actually a better predictor of job performance, or worse?" />

- **Why the bad email fails.** A buried ask, a rambling opening, an
  irrelevant tangent about lunch plans — the same overestimation of how
  clearly the words alone were landing that Week 9's Kruger et al.
  (2005) finding on egocentrism in text already covered.
- **The three-sentence rule.** Direct handoff to the lab's shrink-down
  exercise.

## In-lecture activity

## Conclusion

Both interviews and emails reward structure over instinct — the most
immediate real-world stakes of any topic in this course. You now have a
structure for interview answers and a length target for emails. Coming
