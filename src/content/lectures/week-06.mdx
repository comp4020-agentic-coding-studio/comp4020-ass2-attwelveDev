## Introduction

Everyone place their laptop and phone away right now — no comment needed.
The mere presence of a phone, not its use, appears to measurably affect
how well you can pay attention. Today's scope is that attention and
presence effect only, not screen-time habits broadly.

## Body

- **The mere-presence effect.** Ward, A. F., Duke, K., Gneezy, A., & Bos,
  M. W. (2017) found participants with phones on the desk — even
  face-down, even switched off — performed worse on memory and attention
  tasks than those with phones in another room; the effect scaled with
  how noticeable the phone was.

<CheckIn question="According to this study, does turning your phone off remove the effect?" />

- **Why this isn't a willpower failure.** This is a measurable effect of
  where the phone sits, not a personal failing — the same no-blame
  reading this course applies to Week 8's incident reports.
