## Overview

"Touch grass" is usually said as an insult. This week treats it as a
measurable claim: how far from your usual radius have you actually been,
and how would you know without checking.

## Content

- your own location history, already tracked on your phone, rarely looked at
- the difference between a plan to go somewhere and a plan that specifies
  when
- designing an outing with a real activity, not "get out more"
- what counts as evidence that an outing happened, for Assignment 2

## Case study

Every location a student visited over a semester, logged automatically and
plotted against home. The resulting map was a near-perfect circle with a
400-metre radius — home, the library, and one café on the direct line
between them — with a single outlier three kilometres out, dated to a
single afternoon in week 3. The radius was not a lack of opportunity; nothing
in the semester had required leaving it.

## Reflection

Due 12:00 Tuesday of week 6: measure your own radius this week, roughly,
and name the single errand or event that would have taken you furthest
outside it.
