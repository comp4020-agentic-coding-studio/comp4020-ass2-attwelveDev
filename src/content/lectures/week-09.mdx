## Introduction

The first two sentences of a message, read aloud — now guess the total
length. It was six hundred words. People reliably overestimate how
clearly their tone comes across in text; today's scope is text-based tone
misjudgement only, which explains why overthinking a message doesn't make
it any clearer.

## Body

- **Why text is bad at carrying tone.** Kruger, J., Epley, N., Parker,
  J., & Ng, Z. (2005) found senders expected about 78% accuracy in how
  their intended tone would be read over text; actual accuracy was no
  better than chance.

<CheckIn question="How much better than a coin flip were readers at correctly guessing tone over text, in this study?" />

- **What the six hundred words were actually doing.** A shared class,
  three hobbies, a five-year plan, and a joke needing context the reader
  didn't have — repeated hedges and self-interruptions, all consistent
  with a sender overestimating how clearly the words alone were landing.
- **Reading between the lines.** Direct handoff to the lab's
  ambiguous-message exercise.

## Conclusion

More words rarely means clearer tone — this reframes overthinking a
message as measurable and common, not a personal flaw. You now know why
