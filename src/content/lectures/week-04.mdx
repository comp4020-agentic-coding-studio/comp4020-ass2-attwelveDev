## Introduction

Monday: plan says asleep by 11:30pm, actual is still awake at 3am
finishing an assignment. Tuesday: "will sleep early tonight," typed into a
note never looked at again. Sleep, food, and exercise are the three areas
of life most often optimised in intention, never in practice — today's
scope is sleep duration and exercise frequency only; detailed nutrition
science is out of scope.

## Body

- **How much sleep you actually need.** Hirshkowitz, M., Whiton, K.,
  Albert, S. M., et al. (2015) recommend seven to nine hours for adults
  18–64 — a real range, not a rough guess.

<CheckIn question="Who got seven hours or more last night? Keep your hand up if you also got it the night before." />

- **Jordan's Week, fully unpacked.** Six stated intentions, zero actual
  times: a plan for an early night, undone by 3am; a workout logged as
  done that was actually a walk to the vending machine. The week's own
  Lab breaks down exactly where each plan met reality.
- **Exercise as a frequency.** Chekroud, S. R., Gueorguieva, R., Zheutlin,
  A. B., Paulus, M., Krumholz, H. M., Krystal, J. H., & Chekroud, A. M.
  (2018) found exercisers reported 1.49 fewer poor-mental-health days per
  month than non-exercisers, peaking around 45 minutes 3–5 times a
  week — but more than 3 hours a day was associated with worse outcomes
  than none at all.
