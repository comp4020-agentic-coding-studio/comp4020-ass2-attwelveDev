## Overview

A hygiene routine is a maintenance schedule like any other: it has a
frequency, a set of tasks, and a failure mode when the frequency slips. Most
advice on the subject addresses the willpower failure mode and ignores the
scheduling one.

## Content

- routine maintenance versus corrective maintenance, applied to hygiene
- building a schedule that survives a bad week, not just a good one
- the actual failure modes: missed frequency, wrong tools, no fallback
- reading your own hygiene routine as a system with inputs and outputs

## Case study

Twenty-three unpaired socks, collected from under a bed, a desk, and the
back seat of a car, photographed together. Every one of them is a small
maintenance failure: laundry deferred past the point a matching pair
survived. Week 12 returns to this exact pile.

## Reflection

Due 12:00 Tuesday of week 3: describe your hygiene routine's actual failure
mode this week — not the routine you intend to run, the one that actually
happened — and name the smallest schedule change that would have prevented
it.
