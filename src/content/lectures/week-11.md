## Overview

A workplace runs on a smaller, more formal version of the same protocol
week 7 covered: a message opens a channel, and the reply confirms it
landed. The difference is that a workplace channel has an audit trail, so
a bad opening is far harder to quietly retry.

## Content

- registers: the same request, phrased for a manager versus a peer versus
  a client
- why a first draft is a first draft, in professional correspondence
  specifically
- reading a lack of reply in a professional channel — is it silence, or a
  queue?
- escalation paths: when to resend, when to ask someone else, when to wait

## Case study

The same email, four drafts, sent to a manager about a missed deadline.
Draft one blamed a colleague. Draft two apologised so much it buried the
actual update. Draft three was accurate but arrived at 11:58pm. Draft
four — sent the next morning, one paragraph, stating the delay, the cause,
and the new date — got a two-word reply within the hour: *"Thanks, noted."*
Nothing about the situation had changed between drafts one and four except
which one got sent.
