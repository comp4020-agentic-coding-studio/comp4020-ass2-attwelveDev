## Body

- **The actual comfortable length of eye contact.** Binetti, N.,
  Harrison, C., Coutrot, A., Johnston, A., & Mareschal, I. (2016) found
  average preferred mutual gaze duration was around 3.2–3.3 seconds,
  consistent across a wide international sample.

<CheckIn question="What was the average preferred eye contact length in this study?" />

- **Silence is not a failure state.** A pause doesn't mean the
  conversation has gone wrong — this week's lab runs a deliberate silence
  exercise on exactly that premise.

<CheckIn question="What's one thing people do to fill a silence that actually makes a conversation feel more awkward, not less?" />

- **The small-talk formula.** Name, one hobby, one recent trip. Three
  fixed slots, filled in any order.

<CheckIn question="Which of the three slots is easiest to fill with a stranger, and which one usually needs the other person to ask first?" />

- **Reading the room, diagnose then respond.** Three specific cues mean a
  conversation has stalled, not just paused: one-word answers where a
  sentence was expected, repeated glances at the door, or checking a
  watch mid-sentence. Diagnose first — which cue, and is it just one — then
  respond with an exit, not another question: thank them for their time,
  name a reason to go, and leave. Treating a stalled conversation as one
  that needs more effort is the mistake; it needs an ending instead.

<CheckIn question="Of the three cues, which one is easiest to miss because it doesn't involve saying anything?" />

## Conclusion

There's a real, short, comfortable length for eye contact, and silence
isn't automatically awkward — this de-mystifies two of the most-avoided
parts of ordinary conversation. You now have a number, a formula, and a
way to notice when a conversation has actually stalled. Coming up: this
