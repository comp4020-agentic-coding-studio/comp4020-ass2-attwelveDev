## In the Lab

Last week's Lab was about noticing a device-free stretch happening on its
own and figuring out how to reproduce it. This week is the same noticing
skill turned on a conversation: catching the moment it runs dry, and
knowing what to say next instead of letting it sit there.

**Activity 1 (rotating pairs, 15 min): Small talk stations.** Two-minute
rounds, a new partner each round. Before each round, thirty seconds of
sitting in silence together — deliberately, no talking — then the prompt:
ask their name, one hobby, and their most recent trip somewhere. Rotate
three times.

**Discussion:** Which felt harder — the silence, or the talking?

**Activity 2 (groups of 4): Reading the room.** The tutor quietly taps one
student per group beforehand, without telling the rest, then either
assigns or lets that student pick one social cue to perform once the
round starts: one-word answers, repeated glances at the door, or checking
a wristwatch mid-sentence. Nobody else in the group knows who it is going
in. The rest have sixty seconds to name what's actually happening and
suggest a graceful line for ending the conversation.

**Wrap-up:** Best exit lines from each group go on the board. Then take
three exchanges that stall the same way this week's lecture's transcript
did — technically a reply, but with nothing attached for the other person
to pick up on: "How was your weekend?" / "Yeah, good." "What are you up
to today?" / "Not much." "How's everything?" / "Same as usual." For each,
write one extra sentence to tack onto the stalled answer — a detail
specific enough that the other person now has something to actually ask
about. Test one in a real conversation before next Lab.

## Afterwards

Report back next Lab on whether your follow-up line got a real answer, or
just moved the "yeah, good" one exchange further down the conversation.
