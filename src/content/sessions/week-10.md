## Before the Lab

Bring your last month of grocery or household receipts, or your best
estimate of what you buy and how often.

**Introduction.** Last week's editing skill was cutting extra words down
to the one thing that actually needed saying. This week the same instinct
points at a budget: cutting a fixed amount of money down to the
priorities that actually survive the cut.

**Activity 1 (groups of 4): Budget triage.** Given a fixed weekly budget
split across five categories — groceries, transport, subscriptions,
"emergency" takeaway, and savings — each group must cut 20% from the
total and decide where it comes from.

**Presentation:** Each group shares their most controversial cut, and
what almost got cut instead.

**Activity 2 (pairs): Grocery list.** The tutor hands each pair a brief:
$450 take-home for the week, $70 of it earmarked for groceries, and a
household already limited to two servings of instant noodles for the
week. Draft a real one-week grocery list against the $70 that repeats no
meal more than twice. Then, for at least three household
items, work out how long you actually have before you run out, and set a
standing schedule — a recurring order or a recurring trip — that would
have avoided your most recent rush purchase.

**Discussion / debrief (pairs):** Swap your list and schedule with one
other student for one thing they'd flag before you actually went
shopping.

**Wrap-up:** Keep the list and the schedule — both feed directly into
[Assignment 3](/assessments/assignment-3-adulting/).
