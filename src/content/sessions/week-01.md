## Before the Lab

Bring nothing prepared. The point of a starting check-in is that it happens
before any changes, so arriving having already fixed something you noticed
in the lecture defeats it. The check-in covers all five areas — hygiene,
dress, sleep, conversation and money — and ends with you nominating one as
this semester's first priority.

## In the Lab

| Time | Block |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:05 | Introduction — this week's topic |
| 0:05–0:20 | Activity 1 (groups of 4): Triage |
| 0:20–0:30 | Presentation / class discussion of Activity 1 |
| 0:30–0:45 | Activity 2: Starting point check |
| 0:45–0:55 | Discussion / debrief of Activity 2 (pairs) |
| 0:55–1:00 | Wrap-up, reflection prompt reminder |

**Introduction.** You're not here to fix anything yet. There's no lecture
to apply and no schedule to follow — just five minutes to hear what the
next twelve weeks are actually about before spending an hour finding out
where you stand.

**Activity 1 (groups of 4): Triage.** Each group gets these ten cards,
printed one to a card:

1. Has reused the same tea bag three times this week.
2. Bedsheets have not been changed since move-in day.
3. Eats standing at the fridge more often than sitting at a table.
4. Has not spoken to a stranger, unprompted, since enrolment.
5. Owns seven identical hoodies and considers this a wardrobe.
6. Says "I showered this morning" — three mornings in a row.
7. Has a chair that has become permanent storage for clean laundry.
8. Replies to messages exclusively between midnight and 3am.
9. Has eaten the same meal five nights running and calls it a routine.
10. Has not seen sunlight before 2pm, as far as they can recall.

Groups sort the ten into Critical / Monitor / Stable and must justify their
top and bottom pick.

**Discussion:** Compare lists across groups — disagreement is the point; it
previews that "how bad is this, really" is exactly what the semester will
pin down properly.

**Activity 2: Starting point check.** Individually and privately, complete
the self-assessment first: for each of the five areas — hygiene, dress,
sleep, conversation and money — rate how much you agree with the statement
"I have this under control right now," honestly and against what you
actually do rather than an ideal, using this scale:

1. Strongly disagree
2. Disagree
3. Neither agree nor disagree
4. Agree
5. Strongly agree

**Discussion / debrief (pairs):** Compare scores with one other student at
a category level only — no specifics required — and each name one area you
expect to improve fastest. Nominate the area you will prioritise first.

**Wrap-up:** This is a starting point, not a judgement — the scores you gave
yourself today aren't graded, and they aren't fixed either. Keep them: week
12's Lab has you revisit these same five scores and measure what actually
changed.

## Afterwards

Nothing is submitted from this Lab. The check-in is yours to keep; the area
you nominated is the one the first few weeks of Labs lean on.
