## Before the Lab

Think of one message — sent or received — that sat unanswered for at
least a day. You won't have to show it to anyone, but keep the details in
mind: this week's role-play and incident report both draw on it.

## In the Lab

| Time | Block |
| --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:05 | Introduction — this week's topic, tied back to last Lab |
| 0:05–0:20 | Activity 1 (groups of 4): The unanswered message |
| 0:20–0:30 | Presentation / class discussion of Activity 1 |
| 0:30–0:45 | Activity 2 (pairs): Incident report |
| 0:45–0:55 | Discussion / debrief of Activity 2 |
| 0:55–1:00 | Wrap-up, reflection prompt reminder |

**Introduction.** Quick round first: did your follow-up line from last
week get a real answer, or just move the "yeah, good" stall one exchange
further down the conversation? Then, this week's topic — last week's
ambiguous social cue had to be read in real time, face to face; this
week the cue is a message left unanswered for three days, with no tone
of voice or expression to read at all, just the fact of silence.

**Activity 1 (groups of 4): The unanswered message.** One student plays a
friend who sent a reasonable message three days ago and got no reply.
Another plays the person who left them on read. The rest of the group
watches and calls out the exact moment an apology goes wrong — for
example, "I'm sorry you felt that way, but—".

**Discussion:** What's the earliest point the apology could have been
saved?

**Activity 2 (pairs): Incident report.** Write up a real or invented
mix-up between friends as a short report: what was agreed, what actually
happened, and the actual reason — traced to a specific chat, whoever
routed the messages, or a step with no one responsible for it, never to
any one person's character. One other student reviews your "what went
wrong" line, since that's the hardest part to get right without pinning
it on someone.

**Wrap-up:** Best example goes up as this week's model answer.

## Afterwards

Propose one structural fix your friend group could adopt: a single
channel, a rotating organiser, a standing weekly slot. It does not need to
be adopted, only proposed.
