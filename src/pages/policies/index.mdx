# Policies and support

## Attendance

Lectures establish the baseline; Labs are where the week's material runs
against your own systems and the faults surface. `Unscheduled downtime` happens
--- illness, a competing deadline, a `subsystem` that stops responding --- and a
single missed Lab needs no explanation. A pattern of missed Labs is treated as
a fault worth a root-cause conversation with your tutor, not a mark
deduction on its own.

## Late work and extensions

A short extension on any assessment is available on request, before the
deadline, no justification required: email the relevant tutor and state the
new date you need. Work submitted late without an extension loses 5% of the
available mark per calendar day, to a maximum of five days, after which the
assessment is treated as a missed submission and referred to the Convenor for
a `manual override`.

## Reflections

Weekly Reflections run on a fixed schedule: one prompt per teaching week,
due the following Tuesday. The lowest-scoring reflection in the semester is
dropped before the running total is calculated, which absorbs exactly one bad
week --- illness, a missed prompt, a reflection that did not land --- without
a separate extension process. A reflection submitted after its deadline and
without an extension is the one this rule is built to catch; ask for the
extension instead.

## Academic integrity

Submitted work must be your own account of your own systems: your hygiene
schedule, your wardrobe, your sleep `telemetry`, your budget. Describing a
routine you have not actually run, or reporting compliance you cannot
evidence, is treated as fabricated data and referred under the university's
academic integrity policy. Discussing your approach with classmates is
expected and encouraged; submitting their data as your own is not.

## Content and disclosure

This course is written as satire, and the target is the culture that treats
sleep, hygiene, conversation and money as beneath a computer science
curriculum --- not of the students who were never taught them. If a week's
framing reads as pointed, it is pointed at that culture.

Reflections are personal by design, and they are never read aloud in a
lecture, a Lab, or anywhere else: your tutor reads what you submit, marks it
against the stated criteria, and it stays there. Week 9's reflection asks for
more disclosure than the others, so it is optional --- skipping it costs you
nothing beyond the mark for that single prompt, which the drop-lowest rule
above may absorb anyway.

If any part of this course asks for more than you want to share, submit what
you are comfortable submitting and say so; a partial, honest reflection marks
better than a complete one you regret sending.
